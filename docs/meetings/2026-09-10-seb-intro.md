## The problem in their words

**Origin.** A few days before Per contacted her, Kimberly and Björn attended a workshop with Pontus in Global Banking whose purpose was to brainstorm AI use cases for that business area; they observed that "väldigt många" of the cases related to annual reports as a data source (06:20, 06:34–06:56). She tried to pack more into the challenge but was de-scoped by Per — "Per diskopade mig och jag misstänkade att ni kommer att diskopa också" (07:05, 07:09). Separately, the CIB Data & AI Hub has itself raised this: it sees "extremt många" AI use cases where annual-report data is needed as an input datapoint, but today it is an unstructured input, and the Hub has already flagged it as something it would like an actual implementation of (19:14–19:39). The case was not invented for the hackathon; SEB will have to go on and solve it "oavsett vad vi kommer fram till" (19:43–19:54). The wider context: the area has a strong AI focus on CRM and core processes within Corporate & Investment Banking, with heavy emphasis on automation via AI (12:22–12:31; speaker uncertain).

**Who needs the data.** Annual reports come in early, when prospecting for new customers, and continuously through the whole customer lifecycle; many different people use them in many different ways (09:35–09:45). Many different business areas in the bank use the data (18:45). At the close: there is a big need from everyone who sits with customers — "ett väldigt tidskrävande jobb att kolla på olika rapporter, dra in dem, hitta nya, göra analysen på dem och ta ut olika typer av data" (33:01–33:05).

**Why annual reports.** Kimberly's primer: an annual report tries to answer how the company performed, why, and what the focus is next; its parts include CEO statement, chairman statement, business overview, strategy, market environment, key achievements, key risks, sustainability and highlights (15:53–16:35). There are also quarterly and even monthly reports, which are not published in the same open way; quarterly reports run on "en helt annan speed", where SEB wants to use parts of the data to give customers investment recommendations — annual reports do not always carry that speed requirement (16:39–17:11).

**The manual process today.** There is no bank-central solution that receives all annual reports, reads them and structures the data for downstream systems (18:23–18:33). How it is done varies a lot between areas (18:41). Step by step, in two variants (18:49–19:05):

1. Go to the company's website and download the report — or have a subscription so it arrives by email.
2. Upload it to a "kärrpunkt" (unverified mis-hearing; plausibly SharePoint).
3. "Så sitter man och klipp och klistrar" — sit and copy-paste.

"Det finns ingen automatisering kring det här i dagsläget. Och ingen gemensam lösning." (19:08, 19:12). SEB's users fetch the public reports themselves; if the team downloads e.g. ABB, SEB or AstraZeneca reports it is replicating exactly the first step SEB's users perform today (28:07, 28:12).

**The input.** Annual reports are PDFs, structured differently from company to company depending on how each chose to assemble them, though with some common themes (18:04–18:17).

## What "good" looks like to them

- **Structured and validated.** Björn: given unstructured data, how do you get it out in a structured way and validate it? You can throw things into an LLM and hope for the best, and that works many times, "men vi vill gärna se hur vi kan göra det mer strukturerat och verkligen få ut kvaliteten i det" (08:05–08:22).
- **Generalisable technique.** Jesper: they are interested in the data as such, but "extremt intresserade" in how it is solved — the bank has a great deal of unstructured PDF documentation it wants structured, so they are just as interested in seeing purely technically how the team solves it as in what the end result is data-wise (10:22–10:44).
- **One part, not the whole report.** Downstream consumers each want only one part: "jag vill se finansiell statement bara" / "jag är bara intresserad av den datapunkten" (20:14–20:33). The implied target of a real solution is structured data passed down to downstream systems (18:29, 18:33).
- **Text and numbers, mapped onto one target.** The data is a combination of text and numbers; the same information can appear as prose in one report and as figures in another, so the solution needs "någon intelligens" to recognise that they are the same thing (22:11, 24:03–24:23). To work at scale it must allow for the same type of data being described differently across annual reports, though with reasonable similarity (22:48, 22:59).
- **Output shape is the end-user's call.** Beyond extraction, and depending on how far the team gets, the end-user should drive how the data is structured and visualised "på ett sätt som [makes sense] för honom" and lets him analyse it (20:47–20:59, 22:06).
- **Demo expectations.** SEB wants a kickstart to see what is possible at the different stages and which datapoints can be extracted (33:12, 33:16). Output format (JSON/Excel/API), a web interface, volumes and language were not discussed on this call (see Open questions).
